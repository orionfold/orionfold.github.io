## Why pair them

Canva is where a launch post, a one-pager or a deck gets its look. It has [templates for more than 1,000 kinds of design](https://www.canva.com/pricing/), and [Magic Write](https://www.canva.com/help/use-magic-write/) can write or rewrite the text inside them.

What Canva does not do is check the words against your own files. When a post gives a number, nothing in the design shows where that number came from.

Flow is for that part. It drafts from your own files, puts a footnote on every figure, and waits for your yes before anything changes. Canva still does the design. Flow is where the words and numbers get checked first.

## How they could work together

We have not walked a Flow to Canva handoff yet. This is the split we suggest, and where each step's proof is.

1. **Write and check in Flow.** Draft the copy with *Expand with Sources*. Every figure gets a footnote to the file it came from. In our [campaign walk](/flow/paths/campaign-with-proof/), all 25 figures matched their sources.
2. **Approve it.** Nothing enters the document until you tick *I accept changes*. The History keeps each version and the run that made it.
3. **Take the text to Canva.** Copy the approved text into your Canva design, or publish from Flow. Canva can [import a PowerPoint file](https://www.canva.com/help/powerpoint-import/), but its help page says charts are ignored on import, so plan to make charts again in Canva.

## The path to start with

Start with [campaign with proof](/flow/paths/campaign-with-proof/). It is a launch post where every number points at its source, in two languages, with every screen shown. That is the text you would then design in Canva.
