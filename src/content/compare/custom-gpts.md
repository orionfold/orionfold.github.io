## What changes

**The job.** Say you serve clients with a method of your own. It might be the way you review a plan, write a proposal, or run a launch. You want it in a form your clients can use with AI, not only in your head. Many experts build a custom GPT or a [Claude Project](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9517075-what-are-projects) for this, and both are quick to set up.

**The moment.** Then you learn something new from a client, and the method changes. Every client who has a copy now has an old one. The next client signs on next week and needs the current one.

**Your choices.** If you already have a custom GPT, you can keep sharing it until it retires, then move it to a ChatGPT plugin. On a personal ChatGPT plan you can no longer [make a new one](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8798878-sharing-and-publishing-gpts). You can keep it in a Claude Project and [share it inside your organization](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9519177-how-can-i-create-and-manage-projects). You can send a PDF. Or you can build it as a Flow Domain, a folder of files you publish and update. If your GPT or Project serves your clients well, keep it.

**What breaks.** The method lives inside one AI service, in that service's format. A Claude Project can be shared only inside a Team or Enterprise organization, not with a client outside it. And formats change: OpenAI is [retiring custom GPTs on December 11, 2026](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq) and moving them to plugins. A moved plugin starts private, so each client has to be given access again.

## What Flow adds

The first change is where the method lives. A Domain is a folder of plain Markdown files. Markdown is simple text with a few marks for headings and lists. Any text app can open it. The folder carries its author, a version and a licence.

The second change is how it reaches people. You publish the folder to GitHub, and a reader brings it into their own Flow with its address. When you publish an update, the reader sees which files changed and picks what comes in. Nothing is replaced without their yes.

The third change is how it is made. You write the Domain in Flow with the AI you already use, and every AI change waits for your review. In our walk, every model run went on a Claude subscription, with $0 in API spend.

## Moving your work

Flow has no importer for a custom GPT or a Claude Project. The way over is by hand.

1. Copy your GPT's instructions, or your Project's instructions, into a Markdown file in a new Flow folder.
2. Add the files you uploaded as knowledge. Flow can import Word, PDF and other files into Markdown.
3. Shape the folder into a path. Give it a front page, a few documents in order, and a worked example. AI Consulting Starter is one model to follow.

We have not yet moved a real GPT or Project into Flow. Keep the original until you have checked the new folder against it.

## The Domain to start with

Read [how AI Consulting Starter was built](/domains/ai-consulting-starter/). Manav Sehgal turned nine years of Amazon mechanisms into six documents in about three hours of hands-on work.
