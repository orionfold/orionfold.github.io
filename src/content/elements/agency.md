## What Flow does

**Every model action Flow has is one card in the sidebar, and it tells you what it will touch before you press it.**
Open Elements (⌘5) in Orionfold Flow and the **Agency** section lists twelve actions, each with a drawing and one line. Its heading says what an action would read, where it would run and on which model: *46 words · selection · Claude Code · Claude subscription*. An action that can't use what you have says why on its own row, in orange, before you waste a run. Press a card and the result arrives in **Review Changes**, highlighted where it lands, with a sentence saying what it did and how much it changed. Keep it, or discard it and the document is as it was.

## The answer first

1. **Open the document in the Editor and select the passage.** With nothing selected, an action reads the whole document.
2. **Press ⌘5 and look at the Agency heading.** It says how many words, *selection* or *whole document*, and the model it will use.
3. **Press the card.** Translate and Convert Table to Text pop a menu first. A card with an orange line can't run on what you have, and the line says what it needs.
4. **Review the result** in **Review Changes**: tick **I accept changes** and press the check to keep it, or press the bin to discard it. Proofread with nothing to fix says *Nothing to change* and opens no review.
5. **From the Reader**, a card switches to the Editor and runs there; the Agency heading says so.

## The job

A model is most useful on one paragraph at a time. That means the one that runs long, the one with numbers that want a chart, the one that has to go to a Spanish-speaking client. Asking a chat window means copying the paragraph out, pasting the answer back and hoping nothing else changed. Agency runs on the passage in place, shows the change against the original, and changes nothing until you say so.
