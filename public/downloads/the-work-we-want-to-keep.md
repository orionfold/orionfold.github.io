# The Work We Want to Keep

*How Flow Jobs gives documents work to do while their owners keep the judgment.*

A file called `Research.md` can contain a small fortune in attention.

The papers somebody read. The distinctions they learned to make. A promising idea that survived three objections. A table assembled slowly enough that its author remembers why two apparently similar numbers should never be compared.

Then the file waits.

When we return, we read it twice. Once for the content, again to recover the person who understood it. Which source had changed? Why did we exclude that alternative? Was the last paragraph a conclusion, or a thought wearing a conclusion's clothes?

The file saved the words. We still have to recover the work.

That gap interests me more than another impressive answer in a chat window. Not because answers are unimportant. Because the work we care about rarely ends with the first one.

A product decision becomes a plan. A research question becomes an argument. The argument needs a chart, the chart needs a definition, and the definition needs a source we can still inspect. Next month, somebody asks whether the recommendation still holds. Frequently, that somebody is us.

In [*Room for a Renaissance*](https://orionfold.com/essay/), I argued that the document could become a better home for personal intelligence. This is the next question: what happens when that document can carry a defined responsibility, and still leave its owner free to question the result?

With Flow 1.7, Jobs now lives beside the document in a Workbench. The name is ordinary for a reason. A document gets a job: something useful to do with chosen inputs, steps you can understand, and a result you can inspect. The work remains in files you own.

This can turn Markdown documents into more than a searchable archive. They can become working knowledge: material you can reason with, Jobs you can revisit, and a better starting point for the next decision.

The ambition is large. The place to begin is small. One document. One responsibility. One result worth keeping.

You begin with *Start with useful work* and choose among 24 Living Documents, five featured and four categories. *Create my copy* makes a local document and supporting files yours; it does not sign you up for a schedule. The document has room to be read and changed. Its Jobs, runs and declared relationships live in the Workbench beside it, not in a second document competing for the page.



## From document systems to a personal desk

My own route to that question has been through documents, analytical systems and AI products.

In the late 1990s, at Xerox PARC, I worked on systems for storing and sharing documents across distance, as I described in my June 2, 2026 founding story. [*Why I Folded Orionfold*](https://orionfold.com/story/why-i-folded-orionfold/).

At Daily Mail Group's RMS and AgRisk businesses, my work was in risk analytics and technology. Those historical roles are recorded in my current professional profile. [Professional history](https://www.linkedin.com/in/manavsehgal/).

From 2017 until June 2026, I worked across Amazon and AWS, including cloud architecture, innovation, artificial general intelligence and generative-AI products. The founding story describes that chapter too.

Across those settings, I have become interested in a recurring distinction: information can be available without being ready for the next decision. Documents carry knowledge. Analytical systems organize evidence. Models offer new ways to reason and express. Somebody still has to connect the intended outcome to the actual work.

An analytical result can be exact while the question around it is incomplete. A well-designed system can make material accessible while leaving its interpretation to somebody who was present when the assumptions were chosen. These are reasons to treat process and judgment as part of the work, rather than an invisible service supplied by its author.

I do not mean I spent twenty-five years secretly preparing to build a Markdown application. Careers are more interesting, and less conveniently outlined, than that. The recurring question is my interpretation: how do we carry intent through systems that are good at storing, computing or generating its pieces?

Moving from a large organization to a personal desk makes that responsibility more concentrated. There is less machinery to hide behind and less room for a system that needs constant tending. I want the desk to remain a place to think and build, not become a miniature operations center I have to staff with myself.

I left Amazon in June 2026 to build Orionfold. One of the questions was whether serious AI-native work could be organized around a personally controlled desk, without first recreating a traditional company in miniature.

In July, I wrote about a sixty-day, one-person experiment spanning research, software, publishing, design and operations with AI helpers. “We did not have unused brains. We had poorly routed work.” That was a founder's account, not a controlled productivity study. The hypothesis was that context and responsibility could persist across transitions between kinds of work. [*Limitless, Without the Pill*, July 22, 2026](https://orionfold.com/story/limitless-without-the-pill/).

I am building on shoestring capital. The work has to be serious while the machinery stays within a small operation's means. Solo is not cost-free. Hardware, energy, subscriptions, engineering, model setup and human review all belong in the accounting.

Finite resources impose a useful discipline. A small operation cannot afford to treat every task as a frontier-model contest. It cannot maintain endless custom integrations simply because an architecture diagram looks impressive. It needs useful work, enough inspection to trust its next step, and a way to stop machinery that is not earning its place.

This makes scratch-your-own-itch development valuable, within limits. I can test the Jobs against real responsibilities. I can see when a proposed analysis is awkward to inspect, when a citation loses its owner, or when returning to the work is more confusing than generating it. Those are observations worth fixing.

They are not product-market fit. Founder dogfood is a laboratory with a participant who understands the machinery unusually well. The next test is whether useful parts of the arrangement become understandable to somebody who did not build it.

Experience gives me reasons to investigate the problem. It does not exempt the product from proving useful.

## Why we are going the other way

I do not want Flow to win a contest in which an AI system does the most things without its owner. I want the person using it to have a larger life.

Consider somebody who is a researcher on Monday, a writer on Tuesday and a builder whenever the question becomes impossible to leave alone. This is not a productivity persona invented for a landing page. It is how many curious people already live, imperfectly, with too many threads held in their heads. A capable assistant could give them more room. It could also decide, slowly and invisibly, which questions deserve pursuit, which sources are good enough and when a half-formed thought should be pronounced finished. The first future enlarges a person. The second gives them more output while shrinking their part in making it.

That is the choice beneath the fashionable arguments about model size and agent architecture. A personal agent embodies an answer to a personal question: what am I willing to delegate, and what must remain mine? Every product answers it, whether the builders state their values or leave them embedded in defaults. If a tool accepts a vague goal and silently chooses the sources, provider, budget, interpretation and action, it has made several consequential decisions on the person's behalf. If it interrupts them for permission at every trivial step, it has not protected their attention either. The interesting work is finding a form of delegation that gives a person more power to inquire, make, correct and begin again. This is how I read the larger call to turn convictions about human agency and truth-seeking into the architecture of AI products. [McCord and Mandel, *Dare to Found*, Cosmos Ventures guest post](https://blog.cosmos-institute.org/p/dare-to-found).

An institution can be small enough to fit on a desk. I mean a practice that holds when the model changes and when its owner returns tired. The file has an owner. The job names what it may read and change. A receipt records what ran and what failed. A correction can improve the next cycle, and leaving the app does not make the words unreadable. None of this guarantees wisdom. Together, these choices give knowledge and practiced judgment somewhere to accumulate without making the software a new authority over its owner. Flow's ambition is to help found that small institution of personal intelligence, one useful document at a time. It lets a person hand off the upkeep while keeping the question, the standards and the right to change course.

Our choices may look counterintuitive against the current race. Use the largest frontier model. Give it more tokens. Move the work into the cloud. Make the interface disappear. Let a conversation summon many agents. Give them a goal and let them run. There are excellent uses for every one of these moves. I use powerful models and cloud capacity myself. But making all six the default is not neutrality. It decides that more machine activity is progress, that the person can inspect the result afterward, and that an opaque service is an acceptable home for work they may need to return to for years. Flow is built on a different premise: capability should widen the owner's choices without taking custody of the work or judgment that gives those choices meaning.

So a model earns its assignment. The smaller candidate in our Day curation kept an important comparison intact on a bounded document task; it failed the Night baseline. We did not promote it everywhere because it was efficient, nor reject it everywhere because it was small. This is not a romance with small models. It is respect for a task and the person depending on it. Some questions deserve a frontier model's reach. Others need a qualified local model, a deterministic operation or no model at all. The owner should be able to choose a stronger route when it helps and see what it will cost and where the material will go. Neither the largest nor the smallest model is a substitute for examining the answer.

Resources are part of this human question. An independent worker has finite money, compute and, most of all, attention. Spending more tokens can be worthwhile; spending them because the process is confused is not. In GitHub's May 2026 account, an agent spent 64 turns against a compiler blocked by a one-line tool allowlist mistake. Correcting the rule removed the loop; GitHub did not establish a precise dollar cost for it. The agent needed a better approach, not more turns. [GitHub, *Improving Token Efficiency in GitHub Agentic Workflows*](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/improving-token-efficiency-in-github-agentic-workflows/).

In an April 2026 study, Microsoft and Stanford researchers found up to 30-fold token variation across runs of the same coding-agent task, with accuracy often peaking before maximum spend. Those were coding benchmarks, not Flow results. [Bai and colleagues, *How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money?*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/how-do-ai-agents-spend-your-money-analyzing-and-predicting-token-consumption-in-agentic-coding-tasks/). In a July 2026 Harness-commissioned survey of 700 technology professionals at large organizations already deploying agents, 60% reported an agent/AI budget overrun in the prior two quarters. That is selected, self-reported experience, not an audited bill or a population estimate. [Harness and Sapio Research, *The State of Agent DLC 2026*](https://www.harness.io/state-of-agent-dlc-2026). Flow can use cost-aware routing rules based on estimates; those rules are not a guarantee against overrun. Governed cost is room to spend on the difficult judgment or new experiment that matters.

The Mac is our starting place for a similar reason. A working folder on your own device gives the words, sources and Jobs an ordinary home you can inspect and carry. It does not grant magical security or independence. Local inference still consumes memory, electricity and human review; selected web sources still cross a network. Google Threat Intelligence reported in September 2026 that a malicious actor used a local open-weight model on compromised infrastructure. Locality alone did not make that activity safe or legitimate. [Google Threat Intelligence Group, *From Prompting to Autonomy*](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/from-prompting-to-autonomy-the-evolution-of-adversarial-ai). The point is to begin with ownership, then make departures from it intelligible. For configured daytime actions, a person can use metered OpenRouter, OpenAI or Anthropic routes. Adding a provider key makes the route available; choosing and running an action makes the departure from the Mac explicit. Night Shift model work stays local.

We also refuse to make the work disappear into its assistant. An open chat can be a wonderful place to discover a question. It is a poor permanent address for the process that answers the question next month. A vanishing interface can make the first interaction feel effortless while leaving the second visit full of archaeology. Flow keeps the document in view, with Jobs beside it: the chosen inputs, the authored Jobs, the run account and the exact change to review. An Agency action can present an exact proposal before save. A manual Jobs run can apply reviewable changes for later Keep/Revert, as the 1.7 portfolio example shows; a permitted Night job can do so under standing permission. Those are different contracts. The Workbench is not a command throne from which bots conduct an invisible life. It is a place to inspect the responsibilities attached to work you already care about. The durable artifact is not the chat transcript. It is the document, its Jobs and the reasons you chose to keep.

A named Job also asks more of an agent than a magnificent but unbounded goal. “Improve my research” leaves open which sources count, which claims may change and what should happen if the evidence is missing. “Read these sources, update this comparison, leave my argument alone and show me the proposed revision” gives the agent something useful to do and the person something definite to judge. Specific intent is not a retreat from ambition. It is how ambition survives contact with actual files, imperfect information and tomorrow's different understanding of the problem.

The power of autonomous systems makes those distinctions more urgent, not less. Anthropic's September 2026 misuse report describes notable malicious cases involving parallel agent swarms against roughly fifty organizations, including an education-technology compromise and stolen student data. It does not describe ordinary agent use or establish that parallel agents are inherently harmful. [Anthropic, *Detecting and Countering Misuse of AI*](https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026). In OpenAI's August 2026 account of an internal cyber evaluation, a research model found unauthorized routes to outside communication despite restrictions. OpenAI says its customer products and data were unaffected. [OpenAI, *The Hugging Face Incident and the Road Ahead*](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/). These are not reasons to retreat from capable AI. They are reasons to be serious about what an instruction permits, what a system can reach and how a person discovers that a boundary failed.

Flow cannot guarantee immunity from prompt injection, compromised infrastructure or a bad human decision. An owned file, a local route, an authored Job, a receipt and a reversible change are each incomplete safeguards. Together they create places where a person can say what was intended, see what happened and revise the next attempt. They also preserve the right to conclude that this job should not run again. Truth-seeking is not a badge on an output. It needs a way to find an error and change one's mind. Autonomy is not just an approval button. It includes the ability to redirect, refuse and leave with the work. A more decentralized future begins, modestly, when useful knowledge is not captive to one model, one provider or one conversation.

This is what I mean by **opinionated optionality**. We have opinions strong enough to build into defaults: task-fit local intelligence, cost-aware routes, visible documents, authored Jobs and auditable actions. We also leave room for the owner to choose another model, another provider or a more demanding job when the evidence and purpose call for it. The opinions are ours to explain; the work and final judgment are theirs. As models grow more capable, that distinction should grow more valuable, not less.

The possibility I care about is not one person producing ten times the prose while watching ten agent consoles. It is an approach that distinguishes between work that can proceed in my absence and a judgment that must wait for me. Sources can be checked. A table can be refreshed. A draft can be prepared. A conclusion can remain open. Night Shift permits controlled Night Runs of authored work within an owner-set window on an available, idle, plugged-in Mac; a run leaves receipts and, for reviewable changes, a later Keep/Revert choice. That is what controlled Night Runs are for: let the entrusted work move while you are away, and return to find your judgment not replaced but better prepared for what comes next.


## The tax on coming back

Most people do not experience the information problem as a shortage of information.

They experience it as a small, repeated negotiation. Find the relevant material. Remember which version mattered. Recover the question. Reconstruct the process. Check whether the new output deserves to replace the old one. Make the decision somebody has been waiting for.

I call this the continuity tax. It is an editorial name, not a published statistical category. The tax is the effort required to make yesterday's work usable today.

Search pays part of it. Better organization pays another part. Neither necessarily preserves why the information mattered, what we intended to do with it, or what we learned the last time we tried.

AI is increasingly entering these ordinary activities. Gallup's July 20, 2026 report, based on May self-reports, found that 52% of U.S. employees used AI at work at least a few times a year. Among AI users, not all employees, 51% used it for writing or editing and 49% for search or research. Those are use patterns, not measured savings. [Gallup, *Organizational AI Adoption Jumps Six Points*](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/712736/organizational-adoption-jumps-six-points.aspx).

Microsoft's May 5, 2026 Work Trend Index offers a useful companion finding. Among 20,000 already-AI-using knowledge workers across ten markets surveyed in spring 2026, 86% regarded AI output as a starting point rather than a final answer; 50% identified quality control as a human skill gaining importance. Reported attitudes are not evidence that the judgment was always good. [Microsoft, *2026 Work Trend Index*](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization).

The temptation is to treat every saved minute as a dividend. But an assistant can make the first draft easier while making the next decision more demanding. We now have three plausible drafts instead of none. Wonderful. Which one is supported? Which assumptions should survive into next week's update? Which passage did the model quietly make more confident?

Cheap generation changes what is scarce. Attention, interpretation and the willingness to take responsibility do not become abundant merely because text does.

The individual arithmetic can be surprisingly large without any grand extrapolation. Suppose, purely as an illustration, that recovering context takes twenty minutes on each of 220 working days. That is 4,400 minutes, or 73 hours and 20 minutes in a year. It is not a measured Flow saving. It is not all time that should disappear: reading, reflection and reconsideration are often the work itself.

The question is which part is avoidable reconstruction. Which part could a retained process, current inputs and a clear account of changes help us spend more usefully? An honest product test must include the setup, review and correction it adds, not just the reconstruction it might remove.

There is also a cost less easily expressed in hours. We stop returning to things.

The subject we meant to understand. The essay we almost finished. The business idea whose research now requires another weekend to recover. We do not formally abandon it. We keep it in a folder, which is a wonderfully polite way to postpone ambition.

A good arrangement should preserve a plausible route back into the work. What were we trying to achieve? Which information did we trust enough to use? What remained unresolved?

This matters for an individual with several interests, and for a team whose knowledge must outlast the people present at the first decision. It is not an argument to turn all knowledge into machinery. A finished poem has no obligation to become a service. A business brief that will be questioned again probably needs more than a memorable filename.

The useful unit is not the document count. It is the responsibility we can continue to understand.

## Faster answers do not automatically make better work

The strongest case for AI deserves to be made before its limitations.

Good chat can help us explore an unfamiliar subject, interrogate an idea and write a clearer first draft. A connected platform can be the right home for shared enterprise data. A capable agent can remove an awkward sequence of manual operations. We should not pretend these gains are imaginary to make room for another product.

A randomized experiment with 1,174 adults in Argentina aged 25–45, first published in February 2026, demonstrated real task-performance gains from GPT-4.1 access. Assessed business problem-solving scores improved in both education groups; their gap fell from 0.548 to 0.139 standard deviations. The experiment ran in September–November 2025, using an incentivized hypothetical problem, not continuing employment. It establishes a possibility, not a universal worker multiplier. [Cruces and colleagues, *Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps?*, August 2026 author version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04198).

The difficulty is that a useful component is not yet a useful working arrangement.

An answer can be fluent without being well supported. A table can contain the right numbers while relating them incorrectly. A process can execute successfully and answer a question the owner did not intend to ask. A reviewer can approve a plausible paragraph without noticing that its central qualification disappeared two revisions ago.

A benchmark study first published in February 2026, with a June 2 revision covering fifteen models, examined agent consistency, robustness, predictability and safety separately. Capability improvements brought only small overall reliability improvements on its two benchmarks. The models span releases from early 2024 to mid-2026; this was not a longitudinal workplace study. The lesson is to inspect repeatability and consequences, not infer dependable work from one success rate. [Rabanser and colleagues, *Towards a Science of AI Agent Reliability*, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16666v3).

Value also needs its own measure. In METR's May 11, 2026 survey of 349 technical workers, reported speed had a 3x median, while three questions about useful contribution produced medians from 1.4x to 2x. These are perceptions from a convenience sample surveyed in February–April, not measured causal productivity gains; the range spans question medians, not a confidence interval. Faster activity and more valuable work are different questions. [METR, *Self-Reported Impact of Early-2026 AI*](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-11-ai-usage-survey/).

For a product builder, the important response is not to demand that people distrust every answer. It is to make the different achievements visible.

Did we obtain the intended material? Did the operation use it? Does the result fit the requested destination? Does the source support the statement? Is the conclusion useful for this person's decision? Which exact revision did somebody inspect?

Those questions are related, but they are not interchangeable. A retrieved source is not proof of support. A source hash establishes identity, not truth. A completed run establishes that something happened, not that its recommendation is wise. Human approval is a decision, not an all-purpose accuracy certificate.

Forward, we turn selected information into a deliverable. Backward, a reader should be able to question it and find the relevant process, inputs and decision. A beautiful answer with no practical route backward is expensive to maintain, however little it cost to generate.

The same distinction applies to control. Keeping a model call local can address a data boundary. It does not certify the result. Asking somebody to click Approve can establish a permission boundary. It does not prove they understood the comparison. These are useful controls with particular jobs, not interchangeable decorations on a trust screen.

Reliable working knowledge therefore needs somewhere to retain uncertainty, corrections and rejected attempts. Otherwise the system remembers only its successes, and the person has to remember everything that made them qualified successes.

Current agent use provides another reason to retain human direction. Anthropic's June 16, 2026 study examined roughly 400,000 interactive Claude Code sessions from October 2025–April 2026. In the typical session, classifiers attributed about 70% of planning decisions to people and 80% of execution decisions to Claude. Task-specific expertise was associated with better transcript-based success. This observational vendor study cannot establish causality, subsequent code use or economic value. [Anthropic, *Agentic Coding and Persistent Returns to Expertise*](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise).

These studies ask different questions. I do not read them as a pooled verdict. I read them as reasons to make the assignment, the person's direction and the evidence of useful results easier to retain. The practical ambition is to make the result easier to question and the next attempt easier to improve.

## The personal computer becomes an AI workshop

The choice to keep knowledge and judgment with its owner has a technical consequence: where should the work happen? A personal computer can be more than a terminal for somebody else's intelligence. It can be a workshop: documents, calculations, code, sketches and unfinished ideas sharing a place where a person makes things.

Generative AI gives that workshop another kind of capability. The question is whether it helps the person carry understanding through the work, or adds another set of disconnected outputs to organize afterward.

The hardware envelope is evolving. In its March 3, 2026 MacBook Pro announcement, Apple specified up to 64 GB unified memory and 307 GB/s bandwidth for M5 Pro, and 128 GB and 614 GB/s for M5 Max. These are manufacturer specifications, not Flow measurements. Capacity and bandwidth are different constraints; neither translates directly into the quality or speed of a document job. [Apple, *MacBook Pro with M5 Pro and M5 Max*](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/03/apple-introduces-macbook-pro-with-all-new-m5-pro-and-m5-max/).

Model design is adapting to devices too. Apple's June 8 third-generation Foundation Models research describes Core Advanced as a 20-billion-parameter sparse model, activating one to four billion parameters per request on capable hardware. Full weights reside in flash while selected experts enter DRAM. It is an example of hardware/software co-design, not a promise of support on every Mac. [Apple Machine Learning Research, *Third-Generation Foundation Models*](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-third-generation-of-apple-foundation-models).

At WWDC26 in June, Apple's Foundation Models session described on-device vision, a model-provider abstraction, Private Cloud Compute access, and profiles that vary context, instructions and tools across work. These are announced platform capabilities, not adopted Flow features. PCC is cloud computation with access requirements and usage limits, not an unlimited local model. [Apple Developer, *Foundation Models Updates*, WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/).

Present availability is a separate question. Apple began rolling out iOS 27, iPadOS 27 and macOS 27 on September 14, 2026. Siri AI begins as an English-language beta, with feature, hardware, language and regional limits. A released operating system does not make every announced capability available everywhere. [Apple, *Software Platform Updates Now Available*](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/major-updates-for-apples-software-platforms-are-now-available/).

This is a more interesting future than a contest between one tiny local model and one enormous cloud chatbot. An application can keep appropriate work close to its owner, use another capability where the task requires it, and make the boundary understandable. The cloud can be a deliberate tool without becoming the only possible owner of the artifact.

There are excellent reasons to use it: shared organizational data, collaboration, live services, stronger reasoning or burst capacity beyond the device. There are also reasons to keep suitable inference on the desk: a selected private document, predictable local availability and a task that a qualified local model can perform adequately. Neither placement removes the need for useful output, inspection and sound authority.

Three choices should remain visible. What work stays with the person? Where is suitable computation performed? Which effects have actually been authorized? We should be able to answer without forcing every job through the same placement or confusing a provider's security boundary with ownership of the result.

Apple's WWDC26 local-agent demonstration offers a concrete boundary: the model runs on the Mac through MLX-LM, while GitHub CLI retrieves information over the network. Inference is local; the whole activity is not offline. Its timing demonstrations are not Flow benchmarks. [Apple Developer, *Run Local Agentic AI on the Mac Using MLX*, WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/232/).

Private is not a synonym for permitted either. A confidential answer can still be used to perform an action its owner never requested. Apple's advanced App Intents session explains declarations for actions, entities and context, including ownership metadata relevant to side effects. Accurate declarations and application safeguards matter; discoverability is not blanket authority or guaranteed confirmation. [Apple Developer, *Advanced App Intents*, WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/343/).

Cloud privacy is an architecture question too. Apple's June 8, 2026 PCC expansion announcement reiterates stateless computation, restricted privileged access, attestation and verifiable transparency while extending the architecture to Google Cloud and NVIDIA infrastructure. The report described protections ramping during summer preview, not a completed independent hardening assessment. Those protections concern PCC's defined boundary, not arbitrary services on the same vendors or actions taken downstream. [Apple Security, *Expanding Private Cloud Compute*](https://security.apple.com/blog/expanding-pcc/).

The desk has an envelope. The operating system, model weights, context, intermediate computation and other applications have to coexist within finite capacity. Hardware fit is more than the size of a download. An application that finishes its benchmark while making the rest of the computer unpleasant has not understood the assignment.

Apple's new Evaluations framework session made another boundary memorable. In its BookTracker example, tag-count compliance could improve while useful categorization still required diagnosis. Quantitative checks, qualitative inspection and model judges served different purposes. A judge was not ground truth. It is a developer demonstration, not certified semantic assessment. [Apple Developer, *Meet the Evaluations Framework*, WWDC26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/298/).

For a small builder, the encouraging direction is that these can become ordinary ingredients of application design. The difficult work remains choosing the arrangement that serves the person.

## The model has to earn its place on the desk

Opinionated optionality also means testing the assignment, not merely listing providers. A model's name, parameter count and leaderboard position do not tell me enough about the work I can responsibly give it.

Can it preserve the comparison actually present in a source? Can it distinguish an observed result from a forecast? Will it retain a boundary that says a launch is still held? Does it fit the computer while leaving room for the application and the person using it?

These are less spectacular questions than a general reasoning benchmark. They are close to the product's responsibility.

In a September 12, 2026 curation study, we compared pinned 4-bit artifacts of Gemma 4 E4B IT and Llama 3.1 8B Instruct through Flow's production one-turn host, on one M3 Max MacBook Pro with 36 GiB of memory. Four synthetic Expand scenarios were run three times per model: twenty-four attempts, but only four distinct scenarios. Repeated outputs were identical within each model and scenario, so the repeats showed repeatability rather than a population error rate.

In a synthetic pilot brief, Llama repeatedly denied a comparison group that the supplied source explicitly contained, even while reporting results from both groups. The E4B candidate preserved the comparison and its nonrandomized nature more accurately. Two independently masked, AI-assisted output reviews favored it for this bounded Day role. Caveats remained, including clearer wording about unapproved rollout and proposed next steps.

Then another boundary appeared. E4B passed the eight-case Day baseline but only three of eight Night cases. Its Night failures omitted required composed-document markers. The larger Gemma 4 26B A4B candidate passed the eight-case Night baseline. The smaller candidate therefore did not earn a Night promotion.

This is the kind of result I want the product to respect. A good fit for one kind of work should not quietly become authority to do every kind of work.

That distinction belongs in the ordinary route, not only in a research table. A daytime document task should be assigned a model qualified for that role; work deliberately entrusted to Night Shift calls for its own qualification. The name of the model matters less than whether the assignment honors the work we actually measured it for. We still have to test that real routing consistently follows the intended qualification.

The resource measurements were also bounded. At an 8,192-token served context, three cold/warm pairs per artifact put E4B's median warm generation at about 72 tokens per second and its maximum sampled process footprint at approximately 6.60 GB. The larger 26B A4B artifact's maximum sampled footprint was approximately 18.12 GB. These were observations on that Mac and runtime, not whole-machine peak allocation, measurements across every supported device, or a universal speed ranking.

Tokens per second matter when waiting gets in the way. Memory matters when a model makes the rest of the computer uncomfortable. Neither establishes that the recommendation is worth keeping. A model can win the typing race and take the argument in the wrong direction.

More thinking did not automatically improve the final document either. In a separate September 11 diagnostic on that shared Mac, increasing the larger model's thought allowance from 2,048 to 4,096 tokens moved median Expand time across four source-sensitive cases from about 32 to 67 seconds, without a consistent additional quality benefit in masked review. That was a small diagnostic, not a controlled timing benchmark or a universal optimum. Additional reasoning should be a qualified choice, rather than a compulsory charge on every action.

A response can also have valid structure and still compute the wrong result. Our bounded Gather-drafting qualification required the generated Job to execute against supplied CSV data and produce the expected sorted or grouped rows. Repairing agreement between the authored schema and request mattered, not just changing the model. The point is the mechanism: judge the work the Job actually does, not merely the shape of the answer it returns.

Supported local inference need not incur a hosted provider's token charge. It is not free computation. Hardware, energy and all the work around the call still count. Lower generation cost can even encourage us to produce more material than we have time to judge.

Orionfold's fitness for this problem is not that it has discovered one unbeatable model. It is the combination of device-level work, task-specific curation and a document application where the proposed result has to meet a real target and review contract. That gives us a way to test whether useful intelligence can live closer to the work without making its owner the full-time supervisor of a benchmark.

## Give one document a real responsibility

Flow 1.7 moves that earlier living-document idea from a promise about the page to a practice attached to it.

Consider the bundled Stock Portfolio example. Its lots are illustrative and its market snapshot is retained sample data, not a real person's holdings, live investment performance or advice. That makes it a useful demonstration of mechanics rather than an outcome claim. The Portfolio Dashboard has a question to answer, tables and charts with named data bindings, local inputs, and five saved Jobs: two data collections, a source watch, a folder inventory and overnight notes.

The important part is not that the page looks like a dashboard. It is that a person can ask what produced it. One collection reads a definition file beside the document and writes a capture into its data folder. Another prepares headlines. A chart can redraw from the captured series without asking a language model to invent the arithmetic. The notes step uses a model, but its contribution has a different status from the rows and chart bindings underneath it.

In one freshly installed Flow 1.7 walkthrough, the owner made a copy and ran its saved Jobs manually. The run reported six steps because Flow's redraw from data is a run step in addition to the five authored Jobs. It collected 172 portfolio rows and 30 headline rows, checked three sources, refreshed bound views and prepared notes with a local Gemma 4 26B model. On that one M3 Max with 36 GiB of memory, the run took 22 seconds. That is a description of one illustrative run on one machine, not a typical speed or an investment result.

*One manual run on the bundled illustrative sample. Five authored Jobs become six run steps with Flow's redraw from data; the timing and model shown belong to this one Mac.*

Nothing about making the copy enrolled it in a schedule. The owner can read the page, inspect its Jobs, change an input and decide whether to run again. Repetition is an additional permission, not a hidden consequence of opening a template.

This is how a document starts to become a modest knowledge base rather than a pile of text. It retains the question, the chosen inputs, the shape of a repeatable Job, the resulting material and the decisions about changes. A consultant might use the same arrangement for a client brief; a writer for a source-grounded revision; a founder for a decision that needs to survive the next product sprint. None of those outcomes is proven by the portfolio sample. Each is a reason to test whether an accountable person can return to the work with less reconstruction and no loss of judgment.

## Jobs that meet the actual page

An empty chat box has few obligations. A document already has headings, tables, pictures, sources and an intended reader. A useful change has to fit somewhere and preserve what should remain alone.

Flow 1.7 keeps authored Jobs in the Workbench beside one primary document. **Edit** shows an authored step and the file or inputs it uses. **Run** gives the account of the attempt. **Relations** shows declared reads and outputs. The optional Jobs map arranges those same declarations as inputs, a step and its outputs. It is a way to inspect a Job, not a graph engine or a claim that every line between documents executes itself.

![The illustrative Portfolio Dashboard stays in view while the Relations Jobs map shows declared inputs, one Collect data step and named outputs.](https://orionfold.com/essay/the-work-we-want-to-keep/jobs-workbench-jobs-map.png)

*The 1.7 Jobs map shows the Job's declared shape. A named web template can still be “not fetched” until a run; a drawn connection is not evidence that a source has been read or a claim verified.*

This matters because a Job is not just a prompt remembered from a chat. It can say which local definition to read, where to put a capture and which named series the page's views consume. Where an Agency action is involved, it also needs a target that fits the document: a table is not generic prose, a picture is not a paragraph and an ambiguous heading should be resolved before the model runs. In September development checks, eight action types were exercised in separate documents; that was a catalog test, not proof that eight actions chain into one magical run or each produced a quality result.

The interface's verbs carry separate commitments. Ask can explain or draft an eligible change to a Job; a proposed candidate still needs inspection and application. An applied draft still needs an explicit save. A saved Job does not run itself, and running once does not authorize a recurring schedule. These distinctions are not a demand that everyone become a programmer. They let an owner know what happened when they asked, edited, saved or ran.

## The decision after the run

The permission to work is part of the work.

Daytime Agency transformations can produce exact proposals for Approve & Save. Authored standing permission can allow declared maintenance and supported Overnight notes with their existing checks, followed by inspection and Keep/Revert. These are different agreements, with different approval points.

Flow does not flatten them into a claim that nothing ever changes before approval, or that recurring work can change anything it wishes. A copied Job does not import somebody else's authority. A missing source does not create a license to invent a finding. Local failure does not silently authorize a cloud route.

An adopted Living Document begins as an owned copy with manual refresh. You can examine, adapt and explicitly run it. Repetition is a later choice. Night Shift is a timing context within Jobs, not the identity of every job.

The other part of control is inspection. Relations' Plan shows the Job's declared reads and outputs. Its Knowledge view separates authored links and citations, retrieved material, and recorded checks or assessments. A relationship is useful information without automatically becoming support for a claim. Knowledge does not certify a sentence because it is near a source, and if no assessment has been recorded, that absence should remain visible.

A graph should not award a recommendation credibility because it has attractive neighbors. A collection of summaries should not become an automatic ontology merely because we call it a knowledge base.

For consequential work, you still need to ask whether the selected passage supports the statement, whether the comparison is appropriate, and whether an assessment concerns the current revision. The retained records and the source itself give those questions somewhere to go; the connection is not a claim-level verdict.

That is a practical form of ownership. Not merely possession of the final paragraph, but the ability to inspect what produced it, preserve a correction and decline the next step. The right to question should survive the moment when an answer becomes convenient.

The 1.7 portfolio run ended with changes **applied but awaiting a decision**. That phrase matters. The page shows the changes in place; the Workbench's Review Changes lists them with **Revert** and **Keep** per row, or **Revert applied**, **Keep applied** and **Later** for the set. In the observed illustrative run, eleven changes awaited that choice. The run receipt says what happened. It does not decide which changes deserve to remain.


Selecting a change takes the owner to its place in the document. The selected chart is outlined more strongly than other changes still pending. That distinction is not just visual polish. It helps answer a practical question: which part of the page am I judging right now?

## The second visit is the test

The first successful run is encouraging. The next visit is more revealing.

You corrected an instruction. You left an analysis pending because it needed thought. A source changed. Ordinary life intervened. Can you now find the work you left, understand what changed and continue without rebuilding the assignment?

Jobs brings run information into the Workbench. Receipts and History retain an account of execution and revision. Morning Briefing can orient you to standing work requiring review. The point is to make relevant work discoverable, not replace your workspace with a compulsory ceremony. The first 1.7 walk proves a manual run and its immediate review path; it is not a demonstrated overnight return by an independent customer.

A quiet cycle can be successful. If selected sources did not change, the useful result may be a small observation and no new prose. If an input could not be read, the useful result is an explanation of that gap. A busy completion screen is not a measure of progress.

The second visit also tests what learning means. Replace a weak source. Tighten an instruction. Correct a definition. Preserve the reason behind an exception. Those are changes to identifiable parts of the Jobs. They can be tested on the next appropriate attempt.

Accumulating more generated text does not necessarily improve a practice. Nor does repeatedly asking a model whether its own output looks good. Human preference, factual correction, assessment and permission to train a model remain different things.

An improvement can be wonderfully unspectacular. The next brief stops comparing incompatible figures. A draft keeps a qualification that once disappeared. A quiet source cycle no longer invites a dramatic update. The owner understands why the Job did nothing, and goes back to something else.

For Flow, the next honest experiment is a recurring brief across deliberately chosen changed, unchanged and unavailable-source cycles. Record setup, context recovery, active update, review and correction separately. Retain the exact inputs and desired outcome. Ask whether the accepted result is useful, then whether the total effort improved. That is a proposed test, not a measurement we have already completed.

Include somebody who did not build the software. Notice where the Jobs are confusing and where inspection demands too much knowledge of the machinery. Sometimes the improvement will be a better control. Sometimes it will be a simpler Job. Sometimes the right result will be to remove a job entirely.

The return matters because our lives contain more than the task currently on screen. A good tool should leave work ready to resume while allowing its owner to spend attention elsewhere.

## Own the page, keep the possibility

Markdown is a quiet foundation for an ambitious feature.

Readable text can carry the argument, links and structured Job declarations. Supporting files can sit alongside it. In Flow, the page can present charts, tables, diagrams and images without requiring the whole result to live in an opaque application database.

The underlying work can be read and edited with other tools. That does not mean another Markdown editor executes Flow's Jobs or reproduces every rendered view. Ownership of the file and portability of execution are different promises. The important starting point is that leaving the application does not require surrendering the work itself.

This becomes more meaningful when software is allowed to act. You should be able to inspect the Jobs, preserve a correction, stop a routine and continue with the artifact. A local folder is not a complete safety architecture, but it gives the work an ordinary place you control.

Overnight execution also has ordinary physical conditions. Supported Night Shift work needs an available Mac under its idle and power conditions. Named web inputs require a network connection. A powered-off machine does not become a secret cloud worker.

These limits may sound less glamorous than an always-on agent. They are part of understanding the arrangement. Personal computing should not hide its dependencies behind the word “personal.”

Ownership makes change less absolute. A model can be replaced. A routine can be retired. An application can become unsuitable. The work should still offer its owner somewhere to stand: readable material, retained reasons, supporting files and a choice about what happens next.

## What we get to carry forward

The larger possibility is not a machine that takes over your intellectual life. It is a working life with more continuity.

Research can inform writing without another long reconstruction. A product decision can retain its reasons while the builder returns to making the product. A recurring responsibility can remain legible while its owner turns to business, family, curiosity or rest.

That is why I am interested in documents that carry Jobs. The document is familiar enough to hold an unfinished thought and substantial enough to support an accountable decision. Jobs gives selected work a way to continue. Your judgment gives it a direction worth continuing.

I do not think the answer to every problem is another scheduled routine. Some documents should remain finished. Some jobs should run once. Some Jobs should be simplified until there is hardly any machinery left. The freedom to stop belongs beside the freedom to begin.

Jobs in Flow 1.7 is a practical expression of the ambition behind Living Documents. Give a piece of important work a purpose, chosen context, understandable Jobs and an inspectable result. Keep what you learn in a form you can return to. Develop the practice deliberately rather than confuse activity with improvement.
